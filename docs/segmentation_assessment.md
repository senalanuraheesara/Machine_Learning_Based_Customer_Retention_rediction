# Final segmentation assessment

The final notebook uses 1,696 unique customers from `customer_features.csv`; see [population definitions](customer_populations.md). Ten features are used: frequency_count, monetary_total, monetary_avg, product_diversity, avg_quantity_per_invoice, recency_days, tenure_days, return_rate, active_months and purchase_interval_avg. Redundant purchases_per_month and spend_per_month are excluded.

Inputs must be finite and complete. Yeo-Johnson (standardize=False), RobustScaler and feature-wise 1st/99th percentile clipping precede K-means. Candidates k=2–6 use seed 42 and n_init=10. Maximum silhouette selects k=2 (0.689517; Davies–Bouldin 0.923400); the fitted candidate is reused.

Cluster 1 has higher median invoice frequency (9 vs 5), active months (6 vs 4), and lower recency (19.48 vs 29.47 days). Outcomes are observed fixed-window activity, not Random Forest predictions; they never enter clustering. The notebook reports exploratory chi-square association and expected counts, not causal or predictive validation. Cluster IDs are not validated marketing labels.

This supersedes the old singleton solution. Population and preprocessing both changed, so the difference cannot be attributed to one transformation. Clipping may hide meaningful extreme return behavior; temporal/resampling stability and business usefulness remain unvalidated.
